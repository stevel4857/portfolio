I Asked an AI to Film My Memories

I've made a few music videos with AI now, but this one is different. "If I Were an Angel" is my own song, and somewhere in the middle of making the video, it turned into a film of things that really happened to me.

Watch on YouTube

The song

"If I Were an Angel" is from my iron Paul album. Iron Paul is the name I make music under, and I wrote about where that name comes from in Iron Paul: Finally Hearing My Songs. This one runs 4:48. It's a slower, soulful, trip-hop song, and I made it in Suno.

No lip sync this time

I wanted something different for this video. No singing to the camera. I wanted it to feel like a memory film: a young couple at 24, told as his memories. He has dark hair and blue eyes. She's blonde with green eyes. At the very end we see him at about 70, silver-haired and alone on the porch of a modern townhouse, looking back on all of it.

It opens on my iron Paul wings card art for the song, which dissolves into the story. It closes on a faint version of the same wings card, and then black.

Then my own memories took over

I made this with my AI video assistant. We started with a treatment and a shot list, and as the story came together I kept thinking, I know this. Some of this happened. So I started adding my own memories.

There was a girl on a second-floor balcony at sunset, staring off into space, while I rode past below on my motorcycle, looking up at her. There was me in a phone booth in the rain, begging her not to break up with me. And there was her place, a fairly new apartment with a second-floor balcony.

I almost didn't mention that last one. I figured it was too much work to change. I was told to always say, because it's my memory. I'm glad I did. Those details are what make this video feel like mine.

Every shot is AI, but it shouldn't look like it

Every shot in this video is AI-generated. There's no stock footage and nothing was filmed. The only things that came from me are the song, my own cover art for the title and end cards, and a photo of me with my guitar that was used as a look reference for the guitar shots.

The stills were made in Grok Imagine, and I approved each of the main characters before any video was made: him, her, and the older him. Then the stills were turned into video, about 40 clips of 6 to 10 seconds each.

The big goal was to keep it from looking glossy or obviously AI. Everything was prompted like real film: a 35mm Kodak Portra look, a 50mm lens, natural skin, practical light from porch bulbs and sunsets, and real grain.

How it came together

  • A treatment. The pitch for the whole film.
  • A 30-shot list. The shots follow the song's sections, so the story moves with the music.
  • Character stills. Him, her and the older him, approved before any video.
  • Clips. Each still became a short video clip.
  • The edit. The clips were cut to the beat.
  • Color and grain. The last pass gives it the film look.

There are also two alternate grades of the video: a 1970s Polaroid look and a Polaroid Aged look.

Six cuts in about three days

The concept came together on November 16 around 12:45 PM. The first version was done by 2:11 PM that same afternoon, and versions 2, 3 and 4 followed before the day was out. Version 5 and the Polaroid grade came the morning of November 17, and I posted it on X and Facebook that afternoon. Version 6 came on November 18. That's six cuts over about three days, and most of the work happened in that first afternoon.

The fun part: the fixes

AI video does some strange things, and catching them is a big part of the job. Here are a few things we fixed along the way:

  • Moths and breath vapor that didn't belong.
  • His eyes glowing blue in the phone booth.
  • A hug that became a real lift and spin.
  • Smoke coming off the car, and a glitch in a blanket.
  • A photo that was backwards.
  • An arm passing right through a wall.
  • The older him, recast clean-shaven and silver-haired.
  • The ending, changed from him holding a photo to him just sitting and thinking.

My favorite catch came in version 6. I noticed the phone in the booth had two cords. So that shot was redone with one.

A film of something that actually happened

What gets me about this one is that the story isn't made up. The balcony, the motorcycle, the phone booth in the rain. Those were mine. My real memories reshaped the story while we were building it, and I ended up with a film of something that actually happened to me.
